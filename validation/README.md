# Validation harness — Trophy Rarity Forecast

Everything here is synthetic: the practice cases ship no real answers, so the
trend path that decides the final case (f00, 6,510-minute horizon) had never
been scored on real data. This harness scores the trend path across many
simulated finals built from the house's own recording pattern, and the model's
robust parameters were tuned for the best WORST-CASE loss across them — never
the best loss on any one scenario.

## Generator (`gen.py`)

The house recorded p00 and p01 every hour, but the page only changes at
refreshes: the first refresh multiplies the tracked base by ~x6.06 (638 ->
3,868 players) and later refreshes slow down. Each scenario reproduces that
shape:

- **Cadence** (refresh every 2h / 4h / 8h / 12h), each with 120 hourly reads
  over 5 days, checkpoint at 120h, target 4.5 days later (the final's shape).
- **Refresh multipliers** decaying geometrically (x6, then x~3, x~1.9, ...
  with decay 0.45 per step) — the first jump is big, later ones are small.
- **Earners grow WITH the base**: each trophy's earners grow in proportion to
  the tracked base between refreshes, so a rate rises when earners outgrow
  the base and falls when they lag — the same dilution mechanism as the real
  page.
- **Two growth regimes per cadence**:
  - `linear`: earners grow with log-time (players keep earning; rates mostly
    rise after the checkpoint),
  - `saturating`: earner growth stalls (log-time with a cap; rates fall
    mildly after the checkpoint).
- **Two dilution extremes at 4h cadence**: total base growth x0.30 and x0.60
  (vs x0.45 default), to check the model does not depend on one dilution rate.

The 55 trophies in each scenario mirror the practice case: one platinum at
100% (id2, "every trophy"), one near-zero rare (id1, 0.03%), one mid (id4),
story trophies (chapter descriptions, fast growth), grinds (numbers in the
description, slow growth) and activity trophies. The answer at target is the
generator's own ground truth — exact, not estimated.

## Scoring (`score.py`, `run.py`)

`score.py` implements the contest metric exactly: mean pinball loss on the
logit scale over every (trophy x level), 5 quantiles per trophy, rates
clamped to [0.01, 99.99] in logit space. `run.py` runs main.ts on every
scenario, scores ours against the two house baselines the kit discloses
(persistence = last read carried forward; curve = the house curve fit), and
prints the margin vs the best house baseline per scenario, the worst-case
loss, and the win rate.

```sh
python3 validation/run.py --out /tmp/val/harness        # generate + score all 12
python3 validation/run.py --out /tmp/val/x --cases /tmp/val/harness/cases --skip-gen --override DAMP=0.5 SIGMA_SCALE=0.8
python3 validation/intercept_test.py                     # constructed intercept test
```

## What the tuning found

- **SLOPE_MIN = 0.0 is the single highest-leverage parameter.** Worst-case
  0.645 at -0.5, 0.522 at -0.25, 0.431 at -0.1, 0.378 at 0.0 (DAMP 0.85,
  everything else default). Structural reason: the observed history is a
  falling staircase — every refresh dilutes every rate — so a fitted negative
  slope is a measurement artifact of the refresh pattern, not a trend in
  player behaviour, and it is typically far steeper than any genuine
  post-checkpoint fall. The harness includes mildly falling regimes
  (saturating earners) and 0.0 still wins worst-case there.
- **WINDOW_MIN_READS = 3**: 2 is worse (0.410), 4 is worse (0.645). With ~4h
  refreshes the 24h window holds ~6 reads; 3 keeps the short window usable
  on sparse cadences.
- **DAMP = 0.65**: worst-case is flat (0.29–0.30) for 0.5–0.75.
- **SIGMA_SCALE = 0.6**: worst-case flat (0.286–0.288) for 0.5–0.7, 0.321 at
  1.0, 0.394 at 1.2; 0.6 also has the best mean loss (0.208).

## The intercept fix — and what it cost on the harness

`wls` now also returns `mx` (the weighted mean of x), and the forecast line
pivots around the fit's centroid: `lineAtTarget = fit.a + fit.b * mx + slope
* (xTarget - mx)`. On a constructed case with exact linear logit trends and
small noise (validation/intercept_test.py) the fix is geometrically correct:
mean pinball vs truth 0.2854 (old code) -> 0.2048 (committed tuned code), and
all three medians move closer to truth (id1 logit error +0.698 -> +0.583, id2
-0.118 -> +0.113, id3 -0.127 -> +0.104). With default parameters the fix
alone measured 0.2527 — the rest of the improvement is the tuning below.

On the harness, however, the fix ALONE (before tuning) made 7 of 8 cadence
scenarios worse (worst-case 0.6098 -> 0.6449): in the falling-staircase
regime the fitted slopes are biased very negative, and the old bug — pivoting
at x = 0 with the unshrunk intercept — accidentally lifted the far-future
line, compensating for the slope bias. The fix removes that compensation.
The tuning above (especially SLOPE_MIN = 0.0, which attacks the same bias
directly and principledly) more than recovers it: worst-case 0.2861 vs the
pre-fix 0.6098. The fix is kept because it is correct; the compensation it
removed is replaced by a parameter that does the same job deliberately.

## Limits — read before trusting these numbers

- **These are my scenarios, not the house's.** The generator's assumptions
  (refresh decay 0.45, earner growth laws, the x0.30–x0.60 dilution band) are
  inferred from p00/p01 and the rules, not observed on the final's page.
  Tuning to them risks overfitting to my own generator. The chosen point was
  picked partly because its neighbourhood is flat (sigma 0.5–0.7 and DAMP
  0.5–0.75 all score within ~0.005 of each other on worst-case), not because
  it is a sharp optimum.
- **The house model forecast (final call 21 Nov 09:30Z) is unknown** and is
  not one of the two baselines scored here. These numbers say we beat
  persistence and the curve baseline on 11/12 synthetic finals; they say
  nothing about the house model.
- **A sharp late dilution breaks the SLOPE_MIN = 0.0 floor.** If the final's
  tracked base jumps hard right before the target, rates fall and this model
  will not forecast the fall. The saturating family covers mild falls only.
- **The "0.35 vs 1.88" practice numbers in the README are vs an ASSUMED
  answer** (every rate x6.06 dilution, earners flat), not real practice
  answers. They are a consistency check, not a validation. Note the tuned
  model scores 0.694 on p00/p01 under that same assumed answer (vs 0.35 for
  the old code) — because the assumed answer is a world where the base jumps
  x6.06 while earners stay flat, exactly the regime the SLOPE_MIN = 0.0 floor
  refuses to forecast. But the flat-earner assumption is itself contradicted
  by the disclosed refresh, where earners plainly grew with the base. The
  harness, which models earners growing with the base, is the better guide;
  the practice number under the flat-earner assumption should not be used to
  compare the old and tuned models.

## Results — dilution variant (main.ts as committed, 2026-11-20)

The variant fits earners E(t) and the tracked base N(t) separately in
log-time (r = E/N), with the base as a jump-decay model (log-jumps decay
geometrically; ρ = median of the last three jump ratios, cadence = median
refresh gap; future jumps summed to the last refresh ≤ target) and a
count-consistent anchored median. Same 12 scenarios, same scoring, same
baselines — apples to apples with the table above.

### Dilution stress scenarios (`validation/dilution_stress.py`)

The 12 scenarios have N essentially saturated by the checkpoint, so they
under-test dilution. The stress generator adds four SEPARATE scenarios
(decay 0.60, 8h/12h cadence) where N still grows ~×1.7 (8h) / ~×4 (12h)
after the checkpoint. They are not part of the 12-scenario comparison.

| scenario | variant | baseline | persistence | curve |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| s-cad8h-linear-d060 | 0.1703 | 0.1042 | 0.1404 | 0.1885 |
| s-cad8h-saturating-d060 | 0.1552 | 0.1285 | 0.1368 | 0.1045 |
| s-cad12h-linear-d060 | 0.0923 | 0.1835 | 0.1269 | 0.1431 |
| s-cad12h-saturating-d060 | 0.1350 | 0.5810 | 0.2850 | 0.0850 |
| **worst-case** | **0.1703** | **0.5810** | | |

Variant stress mean 0.1382 vs baseline 0.2493. The variant wins the 12h
pair decisively (the baseline's 12h-saturating 0.5810 is its worst failure
anywhere) and loses the 8h pair. The 8h loss is diagnosed per-trophy:
floor-pinned trophies (last = 0.01, answer = 0.01) are forecast at ~0.0155
because a printed rate stuck at the 0.01 floor while N grows fakes earner
growth in the reconstruction (E = rate × N grows ∝ N while the rate is
pinned). A flat print cannot separate genuine earner growth from base
growth — that is unidentifiable from the data, and the baseline wins there
only because flat-in → flat-out happens to match the truth in those worlds.

### Rejected fixes (measured, not assumed)

- **Pinned-rate guard** (span ≤ 0.01 and last ≤ 0.10 → direct fit): fixed
  d060 but fired on 48/55 trophies in cad4h-saturating and killed its gains
  (mean delta +0.0415). Removed.
- **Dropping 0.00 reads from the E fit** (with a flat-only guard): mean
  0.1972 / worst 0.2554; dropping the guard alone made d060-linear much
  worse (0.2442, 9/12) because all-zero trophies lost their E anchor and
  the direct fallback's class-mean slope lifted them. Removed. Kept instead:
  midpoint censoring — 0.00% prints reconstructed as E = 0.0025% × N.
- **Window-base-growth correction** (subtract the window's own ln N slope
  from the earner slope, and from the class means): fixed the stress 8h pair
  (3/4 stress wins) but collapsed the main harness worst-case 0.2512 →
  0.4255 (cad12h-linear). For resolved rates the base growth is already
  netted out of the earner fit through the rate's own movement — subtracting
  it again double-counts. Removed in full.
- **ρ estimator**: OLS of ln g_k on k was replaced by the median of the last
  three jump ratios (early ratios overstate the decay: in the 8h-d060 stress
  world OLS gave ρ ≈ 0.70 vs the true 0.60). Main-12 effect negligible
  (mean 0.1963 → 0.1960, worst 0.2524 → 0.2512); kept on principle, since
  only recent ratios govern the next jumps.
